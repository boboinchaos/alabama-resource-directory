LIHEAP (the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program) is the biggest source of help with power and heating bills in Alabama. The money is federal, the state agency ADECA hands it out, and you apply through the **community action agency** that serves your county. Payments go straight to your power or gas company, not to you.

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Heating season | October 1 – April 30 |
| Cooling season | May 1 – September 30 |
| Income limit | At or under 150% of the federal poverty level |
| Where to apply | Your county's community action agency (not ADECA) |
| How it's paid | Directly to the utility or fuel supplier |

Each season usually allows one regular payment and, if you are facing a shutoff, one crisis payment. Under the state's plan for the 2026–27 year, a regular heating payment runs roughly $280 to $580, depending on income and household size. Crisis amounts can be higher.

## Step 1: Find your county's agency

Alabama has 18 regional community action agencies, and each one covers a set of counties. The easiest way to find yours is to open your county's [power bill help page](/help/power-bill-help/) on this site. LIHEAP is listed at the top with the right phone number and booking link.

One change worth knowing: in **Jefferson County**, LIHEAP is now run by the Community Action Agency of Northeast Alabama, not JCCEO. See [Jefferson County power bill help](/alabama/jefferson/power-bill-help/) for the current number.

## Step 2: Book early

Most agencies take appointments, and many book online. Funding is first come, first served, and slots fill fast in the first weeks of the heating season and during cold snaps. If the online calendar is full, call and ask when new slots open.

## Step 3: Bring the right papers

Agencies ask for close to the same list:

- Photo ID for the person applying
- Social Security cards (or numbers) for everyone in the home
- Proof of the last 30 days of income for every adult over 18 (pay stubs, Social Security or SSI award letters, unemployment)
- Your most recent power or gas bill, in the applicant's name
- A shutoff or disconnect notice, if you have one

Missing one document is the most common reason people get turned away, so call ahead and ask exactly what your office wants.

## If your power is about to be cut off

Tell the agency you have a disconnect notice. That moves you to **crisis** help, which is handled faster. Then call your power company and ask for a short extension while your LIHEAP application is processed. Most utilities will hold off when they know payment is coming.

If LIHEAP is out of money, try these next:

- **Project SHARE** for Alabama Power and electric co-op customers age 60+ or disabled
- **ABC Trust**, also handed out through community action agencies
- Local churches and charities on your [county's power bill page](/help/power-bill-help/)
- Dial **2-1-1** for funds that open up week to week

## Lower your bills for good: Weatherization

The same agencies run the Weatherization Assistance Program, which adds insulation, seals air leaks and fixes heating systems for free. The income limit is higher (200% of poverty), and you can ask about it at your LIHEAP appointment.
