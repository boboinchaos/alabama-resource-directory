Can't Pay Your Alabama Power Bill? 7 Places to Get Help

By the Alabama Resource Directory team · Updated November 6, 2026

A past-due power bill is the most common reason people search this directory. Here is where to turn, in the order that usually works fastest.

1. Call Alabama Power first

Before the due date, call 1-800-245-2244 and ask for a payment arrangement. Spreading a past-due balance over a few months is often the quickest fix, and it buys time while you apply for help below.

2. LIHEAP (community action agency)

The federal energy program pays your bill directly. Income must be at or under 150% of the poverty level. Apply through your county's community action agency. Read our step-by-step LIHEAP guide.

3. Project SHARE

Project SHARE is funded by Alabama Power customers who round up their bills. It helps people 60 and older or living with a disability with emergency power bills, mostly in winter and peak summer. The Salvation Army in Birmingham runs it statewide at (205) 328-2420, and local Salvation Army offices and community action agencies take applications. Many electric co-ops run their own Project Share too.

4. The $14.50 monthly bill credit

Alabama Power gives a $14.50 credit every month to customers who receive SSI or Medicaid for Low Income Families. You have to sign up, so call 1-800-245-2244 or visit alabamapower.com/paymentassistance.

5. Special Needs Assistance

If you live on a monthly government check, Alabama Power can move your due date to match the day your check arrives. It also offers Braille and large-print bills and services for deaf customers.

6. ABC Trust

Alabama Power stockholders fund the ABC Trust, which helps with heating and cooling bills through local community action agencies. Ask about it at your LIHEAP appointment.

7. Local churches and charities

Every county has smaller funds run by churches, the Salvation Army and Society of St. Vincent de Paul groups. They change week to week. Pick your county below to see who is listed, with phone numbers and hours.

Got a shutoff notice?

  • Call Alabama Power and tell them you are applying for help. Ask for an extension.
  • If someone in the home has a serious medical condition, ask about the medical rules for disconnection.
  • Still stuck? The Alabama Public Service Commission takes complaints about Alabama Power at 1-800-392-8050 after you have tried the company.

Not an Alabama Power customer?

Co-ops, city utilities (Huntsville, Decatur, Florence, Dothan and others) and gas companies each have their own help. They are listed on each county's power bill page.

Every program changes. Call before you go, and dial 2-1-1 for anything not listed. See something wrong? Tell us.
