Free Roof Replacement and Repair Programs in Alabama

By the Alabama Resource Directory team · Updated November 5, 2026

A leaking roof gets worse fast, and a new one can cost more than many families can pay. These are the main ways Alabamians get a roof paid for, fully or in part.

Strengthen Alabama Homes (up to $10,000)

The Alabama Department of Insurance runs Strengthen Alabama Homes, which pays up to $10,000 to upgrade an owner-occupied, single-family home to a FORTIFIED roof, the storm-resistant standard that also lowers the wind part of your insurance premium.

What to know:

  • There is no income limit for the state program.
  • Applications open in scheduled rounds, mainly for Mobile and Baldwin counties, and spots go quickly.
  • You pay a small fee for a FORTIFIED evaluation, then get bids from three approved contractors. The grant pays the contractor directly. If the job costs more than the grant, you pay the difference.

Outside the coast, Strengthen Alabama Homes money also reaches homeowners through Habitat for Humanity partnerships, such as Habitat of Greater Birmingham and Habitat Tuscaloosa. Those versions do have income limits and their own application windows. Habitat Birmingham's list was closed this fall and is expected to reopen in January.

USDA Section 504 (rural homes)

If you live in a rural area, USDA Rural Development offers:

  • Grants up to $10,000 for homeowners 62 or older with very low income, to remove health and safety hazards such as a failing roof
  • Loans up to $40,000 at 1% for 20 years for very-low-income owners of any adult age
  • Up to $50,000 combined

Call the USDA area office for your county. They are listed on each county's roof and home repair page.

County and city repair programs

Many counties and cities use federal CDBG money for emergency repairs. For example, Jefferson County Community Services (205-325-5761) offers housing rehab, emergency repair grants and accessibility grants for income-eligible owners outside Birmingham, Bessemer and a few other cities, which run their own programs.

Nonprofits

  • Habitat for Humanity affiliates do critical home repairs in many counties.
  • Rebuilding Together Central Alabama does free repairs for low-income homeowners, with priority for seniors and people with disabilities.
  • Repair Baldwin (Ecumenical Ministries) does free critical repairs, including roofs, in Baldwin County.
  • Faith groups such as Next Step STORM and REPAIR Angels help in the Birmingham area.

What these programs usually ask for

  • You own the home and live in it (often for at least a year)
  • Your name is on the deed
  • Proof of income for everyone in the home
  • Homeowner's insurance (required by some roof programs)
  • Property taxes paid up

If you don't qualify or can't wait

Waiting lists for free roofs can run months or years. If water is coming in now, get a licensed contractor to tarp or patch it so the damage doesn't spread, and ask about financing. You can request free quotes from local contractors here. If you are getting a new roof anyway, ask for a FORTIFIED roof: it can lower your insurance for years.

Watch for scams. Real programs never charge you to apply, and no legitimate grant asks you to pay a contractor in cash up front.

Every program changes. Call before you go, and dial 2-1-1 for anything not listed. See something wrong? Tell us.
