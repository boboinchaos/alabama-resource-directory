Home Repair Help for Seniors and People With Disabilities in Alabama

By the Alabama Resource Directory team · Updated November 4, 2026

Staying in your own home gets harder when the roof leaks, the plumbing fails or the steps become dangerous. Alabama has more help for older and disabled homeowners than most people realize. It's just spread across a lot of different offices.

USDA Section 504: the main federal grant

For homeowners 62 or older in rural areas with very low income, USDA gives grants up to $10,000 (a lifetime limit) to fix health and safety hazards: roofs, plumbing, wiring, heating, ramps and bathroom safety. Younger owners can get 1% loans up to $40,000. Many small Alabama towns count as rural. Call the USDA area office listed on your county's roof and home repair page.

Your Area Agency on Aging

Every county is covered by an Area Agency on Aging (for example M4A in central Alabama or SARCOA in the Wiregrass). They don't always pay for repairs themselves, but they know which local repair, ramp and weatherization programs have money right now. Find yours on your county's respite and caregiver page.

For people with disabilities: ADRS SAIL

The Alabama Department of Rehabilitation Services runs SAIL (State of Alabama Independent Living), 1-844-602-7245. It helps Alabamians with significant disabilities with home modifications and equipment. Ramps are often built by volunteer church or Lions groups using materials ADRS supplies.

County, city and nonprofit repair programs

  • County and city CDBG programs do emergency repairs and accessibility grants (wider doors, grab bars, ramps) for income-eligible owners.
  • Habitat for Humanity affiliates run critical repair programs, some with low-cost loans, like Calhoun Cleburne Habitat.
  • Rebuilding Together Central Alabama does free repairs and accessibility work.
  • Weatherization through your community action agency fixes drafts, insulation and heating systems for free, with an income limit of 200% of poverty.

Veterans

Veterans with service-connected disabilities can ask the VA about adaptive housing grants, which can pay for major changes like ramps, roll-in showers and wider doorways.

Tips that save time

  1. Make a list of every problem, worst first. Programs fund safety items (roof, wiring, heat, steps) before cosmetic ones.
  2. Have your deed, ID, income proof and tax receipt ready.
  3. Apply to more than one program. Waiting lists are long, and you can take whichever comes through first.
  4. If a repair can't wait, get a contractor quote so you know the real cost. Some programs ask for one anyway.
Every program changes. Call before you go, and dial 2-1-1 for anything not listed. See something wrong? Tell us.
